About the work.

Seen at close range, the group is all leaning bodies and interlocked limbs. A man bends down over a woman whose head falls back, eyes closed, mouth slightly open. His face presses into the curve of her chest and neck; her arms reach around him. The bronze is worked in heavy, tactile strokes — dark brown and black with green settling in every crease of muscle and hair. Highlights catch on the man's shoulder and the woman's throat; the rest sinks into shadow.

I Am Beautiful takes its title from Charles Baudelaire's poem "Her hair and her petticoat, entangled." Rodin developed the group between 1882 and 1885 from material first explored for The Gates of Hell, then released it as an independent composition. This cast was produced in 1968 at the Georges Rudier foundry in Paris under licence from the Musée Rodin, with the museum copyright and founder's inscription on the base.

Step back and the seventy-five-centimetre group reads as a single dark mass. Step in again and the intimacy returns — not a display of strength but of proximity, two bodies folded into one bronze surface.
