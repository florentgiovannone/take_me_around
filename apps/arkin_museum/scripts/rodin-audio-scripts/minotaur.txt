About the work.

The sculpture fills the frame with two heads in tight contact. On the right, a young woman's face in calm profile, eyes closed, lips sealed, skin modelled smoothly. On the left, an older, ravaged face with a bulging eye and open mouth, cheek pressed against the younger one's temple. The bronze is dark and reflective, patinated to black-brown with sharp highlights along the brows, lips, and cheekbones. No bodies, no rock base in view — only this confrontation of surfaces at sixty-one centimetres.

Rodin showed the group under many titles — The Minotaur (Faun and Nymph), Faun and Nymph, Satyr and Woman — and modelled it around 1883–1885 in the midst of his work on The Gates of Hell. This Arkın cast was produced in 2013 at the Susse foundry in Paris, signed and stamped EA III/IV, with a rich brown and green patina.

Walk around the pair and the reading shifts with every angle: from one side the smooth profile dominates; from the other, the gaping mouth and staring eye take over. Rodin compresses an entire drama into the narrow space between two cheeks.
