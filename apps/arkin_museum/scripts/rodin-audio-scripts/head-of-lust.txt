About the work.

The head tilts down and turns slightly to one side. Deep brows shadow the eyes; the lips part just enough to suggest breath or speech withheld. Thick, wavy hair is modelled in rough masses; the neck is muscular, cut off at the chest in a jagged truncation. The bronze is glossy dark brown and green-black, highlights running along the forehead, nose, and cheekbone while the eyes stay in darkness.

Rodin conceived Head of Lust in 1882 for The Gates of Hell, where a female figure of Lust appears in the group Avarice and Lust. This Arkın cast was produced by Alexis Rudier between 1917 and 1918, with the founder's mark on the base and a repeat interior signature. At thirty-seven point three centimetres it reads as an isolated study head — heavy, inward, resistant to quick reading.

Stand close and the downward tilt becomes the whole subject. From above you see the crown of the head and the set of the shoulders; from below, the open mouth and the weight of the brow. Rodin leaves you to decide whether this is sorrow, exhaustion, or something held back.
