About the work.

One nude man sits on a block of chiselled stone, torso torqued, both arms lifted behind his head, face tilted back into shadow. A raised knee breaks the silhouette; the extended foot braces against the rock. The body is polished smooth; the base is left deliberately rough, so the contrast between finished flesh and raw stone is part of the meaning. At thirty-two centimetres the figure fits in the hand, but the stretch through the ribs and thighs reads larger.

Rodin modelled this Seated Titan around 1878–1882, soon after his return from Italy, as one of the figures he contributed to Albert-Ernest Carrier-Belleuse's Vase of the Titans. This Arkın example is an isolated white-marble rendition model, signed A. Rodin — not the full four-figure jardinière, but the same Michelangelesque crouch extracted and let stand alone.

Walk around the block. From behind the spine arches like a bow; from the side the raised knee and bowed head close into a single curve of effort. Carrier-Belleuse took the public credit for the vase; Rodin's authorship of the Titans was only formally acknowledged decades later.
