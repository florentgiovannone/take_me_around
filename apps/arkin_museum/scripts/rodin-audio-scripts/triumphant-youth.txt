About the work.

A nude man sits on rough rock, leaning back as a woman drapes across his lap. Her right arm reaches around his neck; his hand rests on her thigh. Their lips meet at the centre of the composition. The bronze carries a rich golden-brown patina, darker in the crevices of the rock and the folds between their bodies. Musculature is modelled with Rodin's characteristic blend of polish and roughness — smooth along the woman's arched back, restless in the stone beneath them.

Rodin showed this group as Triumphant Youth at the 1896 Paris Salon, though he also exhibited it under other titles — Eternal Youth, The Grandmother's Kiss, Old Age and Adolescence. It was conceived in 1894. This Arkın cast bears the Thiébault Frères foundry inscription and stands at fifty-two centimetres.

Walk around the base and the embrace keeps rearranging itself: from behind, the man's broad back; from the front, the woman's thrown-back head; from the side, the narrow bridge of the kiss. Rodin gave the group many names because it refuses to settle into one story.
