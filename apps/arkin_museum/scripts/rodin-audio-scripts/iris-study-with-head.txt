Iris, Study with Head by Auguste Rodin, French, 1840 to 1917.

A headless bronze figure balanced on one leg, the other kicked out sideways, one arm gone at the shoulder — acrobatic strain frozen in dark patinated metal.

About the work.

The figure stands on a bent right leg, left leg extended horizontally. The head is absent — a rough stump at the neck — and the left arm ends at the shoulder. The right arm reaches across the torso to grasp the extended foot. Muscles torque through the abdomen and thigh; the surface is dark brown-green, highlights running along the chest and the outstretched limb. At fifty-three centimetres the pose reads as a single line of tension from foot to hip to stump.

Rodin conceived Iris, messagère des dieux between 1890 and 1891, originally for his Monument to Victor Hugo. He later removed the head and arm from many versions, leaving the headless, splayed figure that became one of his most discussed works. This Arkın cast was produced by Susse Fondeurs for the Musée Rodin between 1969 and 1972.

Walk around the figure and the balance keeps shifting — from the front the reaching arm pulls the eye across the body; from the side the horizontal leg shoots outward like a lever. What looks like flight is held together only by the standing foot.
