A white-marble figure hunched on rock, arms clasped around a raised knee, head bowed — the fourth Titan, shown here as a solitary study.

About the work.

The man crouches low on a rough-hewn marble block, one knee drawn up to his chest, both arms wrapped around the shin, head sunk toward the knee. The left leg hangs down the face of the stone. Muscle is modelled smoothly along the back and shoulder; the base stays jagged and unfinished. The pose is closed, inward, almost self-enclosed — less a figure supporting a weight above than one folded against his own body.

Rodin carved this Titan IV (from the Vase of the Titans) between 1878 and 1882 as part of the group he modelled for Carrier-Belleuse's Vase of the Titans, after returning from Italy. The Arkın piece is a signed white-marble rendition model at thirty-two centimetres — the single figure extracted from the decorative ensemble that Carrier signed alone.

Viewed from the side, the long curve of the spine is the whole story. From the front, only the bowed head and clasped arms are visible. It is the quietest of the four Titans, and perhaps the most modern: a body thinking, not performing.
