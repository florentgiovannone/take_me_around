The Kiss, 3rd Reduction by Auguste Rodin, French, 1840 to 1917.

<break time="1.2s" />
A small-scale, chamber version of Rodin's most famous embrace — Paolo and Francesca caught in the kiss that, in Dante, will cost them eternity.

<break time="1.2s" />
About the work.

At chamber scale, a man leans over a woman seated on rock; her head angles up to meet his lips, his arm across her shoulder, her arm around his neck. Dark lustrous patina runs brown and black with green in the hair and the hollows of the shoulders. The embrace is tight, quiet, almost entirely upper body.

The couple began on The Gates of Hell as Paolo and Francesca da Rimini from Dante's Inferno, before Rodin lifted them out as an independent work.

Le Baiser as a free-standing work was exhibited in plaster in Brussels in 1887 and in bronze in Paris later the same year. In 1898 Rodin signed a ten-year contract with the Barbedienne foundry to cast an unlimited edition in four reduced scales — 72 cm, 60 cm, 40 cm and 25 cm. The 3ème Réduction at 39.5 cm corresponds to the third of those sizes and was cast in considerable numbers between 1910 and 1918.

What survives the reduction is the quiet of the gesture. Francesca leans into Paolo; their lips meet; his book, which Dante mentions, lies forgotten on the rock at her hip. Approach close and you can read the pulse of the moment. They do not yet know it cannot last.
